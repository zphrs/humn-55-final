How Viral Spikes Shape Digital Movements

Before social media, movements met up in person, disrupted events with protests, and made noise in real life. Today, most of that happens online. There are still protests and there are still real live meetings and discussions but those are just the tips of the iceberg. The majority of the conversations and collaboration toward change occur online today.

We created our visualization to explore how different social movements captured the attention of social media users, or in other words, to examine their visibility and reach. This is because traditional media used to operate as a monopoly on public attention - it could decide not to cover a movement for ideological or corporate reasons. Thus movements relied on mass media to publicize their cause and events to tell their story. Today, mass media can distort or trivialize causes of movements.

We wanted to help future movement leaders figure out how to navigate what happens below the surface. What they, in large part, don’t get to see. Sure they see their tweets but that is only the slice that algorithms want to show them. By displaying a dashboard of people and their tweeting behavior, it makes it obvious that these movements are driven by attention-driven algorithms. This emphasizes the need for movements to both be prepared for spikes in engagement and for the hijacking of movements.
